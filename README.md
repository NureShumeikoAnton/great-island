# Great Island

An "island" at the top of the screen: break reminders, what is playing, screenshots and screen recording. Windows 10/11 only.

The interface is in Russian or English: by default the language of Windows (English when it is neither), or a choice in settings. Button and setting names in this document are given in English.

- Collapsed island: rings in the reminders' colors (they start at 12 o'clock and shrink counter-clockwise) and the time to the nearest one. After 10 s of rest it dims and shows minutes.
- Hover: the list of timers and a pause button.
- Holding on a timer row (or ring) rewinds it: it starts slowly (6 s of the timer per second held), and the speed doubles every half second up to 12 minutes per second. 1 s held ≈ 13 s of the timer, 2 s ≈ a minute, 7 s ≈ 50 minutes.
- A reminder: the island expands with a sound, then restarts itself after `durationSec` or when "Done" is pressed. Each reminder has its own sound, or none: Bell, Chord, Drop, Kalimba, Breath, Bowl, Bubble, Chime; each sounds one way when the break begins and quieter, downward, when it ends.
- Away from the computer (no input for `idleMin` minutes), the computer slept, or the app was closed for longer than that: resettable timers start over.
- Timers are saved every 15 s and on exit: after a restart the countdown continues.
- Updates: a check at start and then once a day, only while nothing important goes on (not during a recording, the editor, a viewer, a fullscreen app, a reminder); the daily check also waits for a minute without input. Every check shows a spinner and ends with an answer: a check mark, a notice for a skipped or postponed release, or the update window for a newer one, at once (if something important started during the check, in the next calm minute). "Check for updates" pressed during the check at start makes it a manual one. The window opens in the middle of the screen, fades in and can be dragged by any empty spot; it has the notes in your language: Install, Postpone for an hour (for a day after the first showing, the notice says until when), and for a patch Skip (then it is installed by hand from Settings → About). The installer is downloaded, its signature is checked against the key built into the app, and only then is it run. A setting turns the automatic check off; "About" in the settings checks by hand.
- Monitor, by setting: the island follows the cursor between monitors (only while it sits at rest: expanded, a card, a recording and open windows keep it where it is) or is pinned to one monitor; the pinned one unplugged means the main one. A capture key takes the monitor under the cursor.
- Fullscreen app or presentation, by setting: the island as usual, reminders only, or nothing (the default). The reminder sound plays in any case.
- "Now playing": music and video from any player that reports itself to Windows (Spotify, browsers, VLC…). Collapsed: the mode badges, the cover, the remaining track time and bars driven by the player's loudness (each bar sways on its own rhythm, the height follows the sound itself, smoothed, on a −24 dB to 0 dB scale; a sharp jump of 5 dB or more (a kick, a snare) adds a 0.13 s pop, most to the second bar; the player's own volume is evened out against its typical loudness, the median of the last ~20 s of sound, so the slider doesn't change the bars; idle sessions a browser keeps for closed tabs count as silence; a calm loop when the loudness can't be read; the meter reconnects by itself after a device change, retries five times over ~30 s while the loudness is lost, then warns with PLAYER-LEVEL); 0.5 s after a track change an announce for 5 s. On hover a card with progress (a reported position within 0.5 s of the shown one is ignored, so it moves smoothly), seeking (the line jumps to the click at once and the player's reports don't move it for 0.5 s) and buttons. A click on the cover brings the player window to the front; a click on the title or artist copies "Artist — Title" and the artist line says so for 1.5 s.
- Activities are paged by swiping with the mouse, with two fingers on a touchpad, or with the wheel. The dots at the top of the expanded island show how many there are. Over a notice that belongs to no activity (system notices, downloads, problems, updates, the sound and microphone indicators) a swipe any way, two fingers or a wheel click only take it away: nothing pages and the control center doesn't open. A track announce pages with the player, a reminder and a capture card aren't swiped. A swipe with the mouse that ends past the island doesn't fold it: it stays open until the cursor comes back and leaves, or for 5 s.
- Control center: pull the island down (with the mouse or two fingers); close it by pulling up or moving the cursor away. Buttons:
  - **Sound** and **Microphone** mute the default sound and microphone in Windows (the microphone also mutes the calls device). If they were toggled not from the island (by a key, from the tray), the island shows an indicator for 2 s.
  - **Do Not Disturb**: track announces and pauses aren't shown; reminders and indicators are. Persists across restarts.
  - **Keep awake**: the computer doesn't go to sleep, and by setting the screen doesn't turn off either. Works while the island runs; off after a restart.
  - While Do Not Disturb and Keep awake are on, a moon and a cup are visible in the collapsed island.
  - Under the buttons, one line with no backing: the clock on the left (with seconds unless turned off in Settings → Island; a changed digit rolls up; with "Enable animations" off it just changes), in the middle the time left on battery (Windows' estimate, else the usual drain) or, charging, the time to a full charge (to 100%: a maker's charge limit isn't known), on the right the battery as icons: the fill by charge, yellow from the low-charge threshold, red from 10%, green with the bolt while charging, a plug when on the charger without charging, the plus for Energy saver; the percentage only when "Show battery percentage" is on (off by default). Without a battery only the clock, in the middle. The clock is redrawn as the second turns, only while the menu is open.
  - **Screenshot**: by the button or by a key (PrtSc by default; the combination is changed in settings and doesn't depend on the layout; while PrtSc belongs to Windows Snipping Tool the island doesn't take it: in settings it can be handed to the island, and the installer asks about it on first install). The menu collapses into a shutter: a ring fills over half a second, and the monitor with the island is captured. A key captures at the press and the shot flies at once, while the shutter is still filling. The island itself doesn't appear in the shot. The shot shrinks to the middle of the screen with a flash, lands in the middle of the island, and the island widens into a card. While the card is visible: a click on the text or Ctrl+C copies to the clipboard, Ctrl+X puts it only in the clipboard with no file, a click on the thumbnail opens the shot. Ctrl+C and Ctrl+X work meanwhile in whichever window has focus. When the card closes, the file is saved to the Screenshots folder (changeable in settings).
  - **Area selection** (shared by the screenshot and the recording, on the monitor with the island): the screen dims and the window under the cursor gets a light green frame that glides from window to window; a click takes that window, a click on the empty desktop takes nothing. A frame drawn around a window with a little room (up to 64 points on each side) glides onto the window's edges and the frame itself turns green, and the release takes it exactly; a frame inside a window is taken as drawn. Alt held draws freely, and letting it go puts the frame back on the window at once (a hint at the top while dragging). The frame shows its size in pixels. Esc, a right click or switching to another window cancels. Opened from the island (the held Screenshot button, the panel's area circle), the selection starts with a faint white wave running out of the island over the screen and fading once it has reached the edges (the "Enable animations" setting turns the wave off). **Area screenshot**: hold the Screenshot button for half a second, or the area key (not set by default): the screen freezes at the press (for the held button, as soon as the ring closes), the area is picked on the frozen picture, and the shot flies straight to the island from where the area was, with no stop at the center and a thin white edge that sets it apart from the screen. **Area recording**: the pre-start panel's area circle; the area is picked on the live screen, the panel comes back with "Area 1280×720" and a lit circle (a click on it returns to the whole screen). Back after the selection or a cancel, the panel waits for the cursor: it closes the usual way only once the cursor has been on the island, and Esc closes it at once. The area is for one recording. Red corner marks outside the area show it from the moment it is picked (a preview while the panel is open) through the countdown and the recording, closing in from the screen's corners in half a second and flying back to them when the area is dropped, the panel closes or the recording ends; they aren't in the video and can be turned off in settings ("Show frames when recording"). The selection and the marks are told to Windows as not fullscreen apps, so the island doesn't step aside for them.
  - **Viewer window**: the shot flies from the card into a frameless window (or expands at once if clicked while it is at the middle of the screen). On a dark strip at the top there are capsules: show in folder, copy, edit, delete for good; in the middle the file name, and a click copies the path. The wheel zooms, a double click toggles pixel for pixel, and the zoomed shot is dragged with the mouse. Esc closes, Ctrl+C copies, Ctrl+X leaves the shot only in the clipboard (the file is deleted) and the shot flies back into the island. By setting the strip may be absent: then dark capsules lie right on the shot, with no file name. While the window is open the island shows its status (it doesn't dim in it): a click on the thumbnail brings the window to the front, the arrow returns the shot to the island, into an ordinary card, and the file stays.
  - **Editor** ("Edit"): brush, marker, arrow, frame, pixels, crop, color and thickness, undo and redo, "Save" and "Save copy". Marks on screen and in the file are drawn by the same code. Unsaved edits raise no dialogs: the first Esc makes white glints run over "Save", the second discards the edits. Edits that change nothing overwrite nothing. The zoomed shot is moved with two fingers on a touchpad or with the mouse with Space held. On the island the arrow in the editor must be held.
  - **Screen recording**: by the button or by its own key (not set by default): the monitor with the island is recorded, and the island itself isn't in the video. A click on the control-center button opens the pre-start panel in its place (the button flies into its start circle, and a click on that circle starts the recording): sound (off / computer / microphone / both; the arrow on the microphone positions opens the list of microphones, the checked one is listened to and its bars jump), a countdown of 0, 3 or 5 s, the voice/computer balance slider on that page when both are recorded, and under "⋯" quality (Low = ½, Medium = ¾, High = the screen's size), codec (H.264 or HEVC; HEVC that the video card can't encode falls back to H.264 with `REC-HEVC`), 30 or 60 frames per second and the dissolve after a pause. The choices are saved at once; the record key records at once with them. A countdown shows on the island (a click starts at once, Esc cancels). During recording the island doesn't dim, doesn't expand on hover and doesn't change width: a red dot, REC, the time, pause and stop; the time grows at the expense of the gaps around it. After a pause the frame dissolves smoothly into the new one (a panel choice, on by default). On hover the island widens to the sides: the file size on the left, a track with a ✕ on the right: slide the circle to the edge and the recording is deleted. The control-center button shows the time and also stops it. After stopping there is a card like the screenshot's, with the length and size in the title (`Запись экрана · 0:42 · 14 МБ`, i.e. "Screen recording · 0:42 · 14 MB"): the thumbnail with ▶ and the duration opens the video window (below), a click on the text or Ctrl+C puts the file in the clipboard, Ctrl+X only in the clipboard (the file moves from the folder to a temporary one). Files go to `Видео\Записи экрана` (Videos\Screen Recordings), MP4, with a sound track (silent when sound is off: without a track Telegram sends the video as a GIF); frames are written only when the screen changes. Sound is off by default. The island's own sounds are left out (Windows 10 2004+); the microphone is the chosen one, or the Windows default (also when the chosen one isn't plugged in). If the microphone is wanted but missing or blocked, the first press only warns (`REC-MIC`) and says to press again, the next one records without it. Losing the microphone or the computer's sound mid-recording is a warning; the recording goes on, switching to a new default microphone if there is one. Capture and encoding are on the video card, with no third-party libraries.
  - **Video window**: the recording's last frame flies from the card into a window of its own (about 80% of the work area, the video's proportions, never larger than pixel for pixel), and the video plays a moment after it lands. On top, the screenshot viewer's panel: Show in folder, Copy (the file, to paste into a messenger), Edit (the video editor, below), Delete (the file, for good), the file name (a click copies the path). At the bottom, left to right: ▶/❚❚ (↻ at the end), −5 s, +5 s, sound (the cursor on it slides out a volume slider; a click mutes), the time, the progress bar (the cursor on it thickens it and shows the time under the cursor; drag to seek), the length (a click switches it to the time left), the speed (a click unfolds 0.5×–4× in a row out of the button, the bar shortening smoothly; it folds back once a step is chosen or half a second after the cursor leaves), frame to screenshot (the frame goes to the clipboard and the Screenshots folder), full screen. With the strip look the panels are dark strips above and below the video (the window is taller by them), in full screen over its top and bottom edges; without it, capsules on the video. While playing, panels that lie on the video go 2.5 s after the mouse stops (the strips slide out, the capsules fade; in full screen the cursor too); paused or ended, they stay. A click on the video pauses, a drag moves the window, the wheel zooms as in the screenshot viewer; a press held still plays at 2× until let go, with a "2× ▶▶" sign at the top. A pause or play from the video, Space or the island flashes ▶ or ❚❚ in the middle of the video. Full screen grows the video from the window to the monitor and shrinks it back, with no window border along the screen's edges. Keys: Space, ←/→ 5 s, `,`/`.` a frame while paused, F full screen, Esc leaves full screen or closes, Ctrl+C copies the file, Ctrl+Shift+C the frame. Minimized, the video pauses and goes on when shown. The volume and the length/time-left choice are remembered. While the window is open the island shows its status as for a screenshot, with ▶/❚❚ on its left: the arrow pauses the video and sends its frame back into the card. Playback is Windows' Media Engine decoding and presenting on the video card: our process does nothing per frame. A video Windows can't decode (an HEVC recording without the HEVC extension) closes with `REC-OPEN`; one whose sound can't be decoded plays silent with `VIDEO-SOUND`.
  - **Video editor** ("Edit" in the video window): the panels swap. On top Back, Undo, Redo, full screen · scissors, the fragment's sound, speed (1×–8×, a row of steps), zoom, transition (the tools all one width) · compression (As is, For chat, Feather up to 10 MB, each with its size estimate), Save copy and Save. At the bottom ▶/❚❚, the time of the result to a tenth, the ruler and the timeline: one block per fragment with its thumbnails and the sound's wave (both read in the background, so the timeline shows at once, a glint running over grey blocks until they come), badges for speed, zoom and muted sound, gaps for the joins with a transition's button. With the strip look the timeline is a band of its own (the window grows down by it, up to 90% of the work area's height; the video gives up the rest and gets black bars at its sides); without it, a dark panel over the video. The panels come at once; the engines open a moment later, a small spinner turning over the viewer's picture until the first frame, which then fades in. Then the window's own engine lets the video go, and every engine of the window decodes on one video device (a 2.5K recording: ~250 MB while editing instead of ~450 MB); on Back the window opens the video again at the caret, the editor's frame standing in, with the same spinner only if that takes longer than the way out. Going in and out is one motion: the video glides to its new place, the bottom panel sinks and the timeline comes up in its place, and back; the window shrinks at the end. Full screen works in the editor too. The caret runs without jumps (a moment lies at its share of the width; a join is a narrow gap the caret glides over right at the join; a transition is an overlap of the two blocks with its button); after any edit the caret stays on the same frame of the recording. Dragging the caret shows exact frames. The caret is shared: a click on a block or the ruler puts it there, and the tools act on the fragment under it (outlined). The scissors are the cursor over the timeline and draw a yellow line; near the caret it snaps and the caret lights up with the scissors; S cuts at the caret, the icon rolls down the cut and the edges part. A block dragged to the start, the end or a join lifts, the others make room; dropped on the "Drag to remove" target it flies into it (as with Delete). Zoom: a frame on the whole video, moved and resized by its corners, "Smooth" (in at the fragment's start, out at its end) or "At once", and apart from them "Reset zoom"; without the strip the video moves up between the panels while its zoom is set. A transition (dissolve, through black, slide, push in, 0.5 s): the tool unfolds a row of kinds, any of which is dragged onto a join (the tool itself drags a dissolve); the join's button picks its kind or "No transition" (or Delete with the list open). Nothing starts playing by itself after an edit. The picture shows the edit as it will be: two Media Engines play the fragments in turns (the next one waits on the other engine), a transition plays both, zoom and transitions are compositor animations. Keys: Space, S, Delete, F, ←/→ 5 s, `,`/`.` a frame, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y, Esc (the popover, the zoom frame, the scissors, then Back). Unsaved edits on Back, Esc, closing the window or the island's arrow make Save glint; the second time discards them. Save replaces the recording, Save copy writes "<name> (2).mp4" beside it: every frame is put together on the video card (the D3D11 video processor crops the zoom, scales to the compression mode's size, lays a transition's two pictures over black) and goes to the H.264 encoder without leaving it; the sound is sped up with its pitch kept, silent where muted, crossed at transitions. While saving Save fills, the edits wait and watching goes on; the window may close, the island finishes the save. Then the editor closes and the window plays the new file. A failure is `VIDEO-SAVE`, the edits stay.
  - **Settings**.
- System notifications, each kind switched off in Settings → Notifications:
  - **Low charge**: from Windows' Energy saver threshold (20% when there is none; kept between 15% and 50%), then every 5%, a small notice with a battery whose fill runs down to the charge. At the first step the island waits up to 3 s for Windows to turn Energy saver on, then says "Energy saver" with a plus in place of the battery's tip. Sound: Pulse.
  - **Critical charge**: every percent from 10%, a medium notice with the time left and a blinking red battery. Then the collapsed island keeps a red battery with the percentage until the charger is plugged in. It shows through Do Not Disturb, a fullscreen app (whatever the fullscreen setting) and an island hidden from the tray. Sound: a falling note.
  - **Charging**: plugged in (chevrons run to the tip, then a bolt; sound Breath), unplugged (the time left; Breath, down and quieter), charged (full or stopped at the charge limit above 50%, once per plug-in and only after at least 5% gained, so the top-ups at a charge limit don't count; a shine passes) and slow charging (a rate that would fill the battery in more than 8 h, below 80%, for 3 minutes). Small notices.
  - **Fast drain**: on battery the drain is read once a minute; the usual one is learnt on this computer (the median of the minutes on battery over roughly the last week or two, kept in `battery.json`; before two hours are learnt, a battery that lasts 5 h stands in). Twice the usual for 5 minutes gives a medium notice with the watts, the time left and the app clearly ahead (≥ 20% of the processor and twice the next); under the cursor it opens with up to three apps over 10% of the processor. Again only after the drain calmed down, and not within 30 minutes.
  - **High load**: on the charger or without a battery, one app over half of the processor for 5 minutes; the same app again after it fell under 30%, not more often than every 30 minutes.
  - **Downloads**: the Downloads folder itself is watched (not the folders inside it: apps keep caches there). While a browser downloads, the collapsed island gives its place to a spinner, the file's name (or how many files) and how much has come, read once a second; the island doesn't dim meanwhile, and a swipe hides the progress until the next download (the arrived file still shows). A file that arrived shows in the small size: the arrow drops into the tray, the name (a long one loses its middle, never its extension) and the size; several in a row join it as a stack of their pictures with the count and the total, and start its 4 s again. Under the cursor it opens into a card: the file's own picture where Windows has a thumbnail (else a placeholder: a sheet with a band in its kind's color and the extension), the name, the size and the site from Windows' mark on the file; several files lie as a pile of the last three with the count and the total. A click on the picture opens the file, on the text (or the pile) shows the files selected in Explorer; either closes the notice. A download is a partial file (`.crdownload`, `.part`, …) renamed when done, or a file written straight that is new, written within the last 2 minutes and quiet for 1.5 s; a copied, moved or unpacked file keeps its old time and isn't shown.
  - **Devices** (Bluetooth): headphones or a speaker connected: the large size with the name on top; the case in the middle and the buds either side, looking away from it, or over-ear headphones and the rest alone, all come up softly in place. The pictures are line drawings of one weight seen through silver: a bright band crosses them once as they come up (with animations off they stand in plain silver). Under them a battery ring with the percentage when Windows knows it (the value its own Bluetooth page shows). It waits up to 3 s for the battery after the link. AirPods Pro, Galaxy Buds4 and Galaxy Buds3 (with or without Pro) get their own buds by the name, other earbuds a neutral pair; every one shares one case. Sony WH-1000XM get over-ear headphones by the name, a speaker its own drawing. Headphones gone: "Disconnected", small, with the device's glyph (headphones, a speaker, a mouse, a keyboard, a gamepad or a phone); a long name in a small notice is cut to fit; a link that drops before "Connected" showed is not news. What is connected at start isn't shown. A mouse, a keyboard or a phone isn't announced: they wake and sleep all day.
  - **Device battery**: any paired device's battery at 20% and at 10% gives a small notice, once per level per discharge (said again after it climbed 5% above).
  - **Sound and microphone switched**: Windows' default output or input changed (a cable plugged in, another device picked): a small notice with the endpoint's glyph (headphones, speakers, a monitor, a microphone) and the device's name (a Bluetooth one by its own name, else Windows' description). The switch that comes with headphones connecting is part of "Connected" and isn't said apart.
  - Every notice but the critical charge stays quiet in Do Not Disturb and over a fullscreen app (unless the island is set to show as usual there); a recording holds them until it ends, and those younger than 2 minutes show then. A click does nothing. With "Enable animations" off the batteries and the gauge stand still.
  - The processes are read once a minute (a few ms); the battery changes and the Downloads folder's changes come from Windows itself (the folder's thread sleeps until something changes; only while a download is coming are the sizes read once a second). `--demo-system` started again shows the next sample notice, `--demo-devices` the next device notice. The devices cost nothing at rest: Windows tells about links, batteries and the default sound by itself; at start it lists every device of the computer once to find the Bluetooth ones that carry a battery.
- The settings window: a list of sections with colored icons (Features: Reminders, Island, Player, Screenshots, Screen recording, Notifications; General: Animations and power, Language, Start with Windows; Maintenance: Check for updates, Journal), under a card with the version, whether there are updates and when the last check was (it opens About). A section slides in from the right and back out (Esc or the mouse's back button go back; with Windows animations off, a short fade). Every change is saved a moment after it is made, and on closing; there is no Save button. The window closes when the island quits. Reminders: a switch and a delete button on each row, the row opens the reminder; its sound is picked from a list that plays each sound on a click (the start or the end of a break). The Journal page reads `island.log`: problems by default ("All" adds the plain lines), grouped by day in one grid (time, a red or orange mark, the title, the code with the reason, Windows' answer, repeats in a row folded as ×N); a click copies the line with the app version and the Windows build; new lines appear while the page is open. "Clear the journal" asks for a second press within 3 s, then empties `island.log` and removes `island.old.log`.
- A dimmed island freezes by default (the bars freeze, the track time is hidden); a checkbox in settings keeps it live.
- Tray: pause, hide the island for 15 min / 1 h / 3 h / until I bring it back (reminders are still shown), show the island always, settings, restart, exit.
- Errors. If something failed because of Windows, the disk, the video card or another program, the island says so:
  - a warning (it worked with a compromise, or a click did nothing): a yellow triangle and a phrase for 3 s;
  - a serious error (asked for something and didn't get it, or lost data): a red disc, the island shudders, 5 s, shown even on an island hidden from the tray;
  - hovering opens a card with the reason, a click on it closes it; while the cursor is on the island the message doesn't go away;
  - a message the cursor never came to, and problems found at startup, leave a circle with "!" in the collapsed island and are shown again 2 s after the cursor next comes to the island;
  - everything goes to the journal with a code (`REC-START/GRAPHICS`): settings → Journal.
- Language: Russian and English. The default follows the Windows display-language list (the first supported language in the user's order, English when none is). Settings → General → Language picks one by hand. The island reads it at start and opens the settings window in its own language (`--lang=`), so a change applies after a restart (tray → Restart); the Language page says so. Names of the screenshot and recording files and of the default recordings folder follow the language (an existing folder in the other language is reused, so switching doesn't split recordings). If Windows doesn't say its language, English is used and the island reports it (`LANG`). The installer asks for its language too (English or Russian, Windows' preselected), asks about PrtSc in it, and leaves the choice in the registry (`HKCU\Software\Great Island\Language`): at the first start the island takes it as its language and writes it to `settings.json`. The journal is written in the interface language as well.
- A crash: the island restarts itself (no more than once a minute), and a running screen recording manages to close its file. A damaged `settings.json` is replaced by the last good copy.

## Design

One exe, two modes:

- **Island** (no arguments): pure Win32 + Windows.UI.Composition. Animations are computed by the Windows compositor, text is drawn by Direct2D/DirectWrite with the software renderer. About 17 MB of own memory with the player on, ~0% CPU at rest.
- **Settings** (`--settings`): Tauri + React, a separate process only while the window is open. The island notices a save by the modification time of `settings.json`.

Code:

- `src-tauri/src/scheduler.rs`: timer logic without windows or the system, covered by tests.
- `src-tauri/src/stage.rs`: what is on the island: the activity ribbon, notices, whether the menu is open.
- `src-tauri/src/gesture.rs`: gestures: swipe, wheel, hold, menu pull, and a swipe any way that takes a notice away (`Edges::dismiss`; which notices: `NoticeKind::dismissable` in `stage.rs`).
- `src-tauri/src/loudness.rs`: evens out the player's own volume for the level bars (a running median of its loudness), covered by tests.
- `src-tauri/src/island/levels_debug.rs`: debug builds only (`npx tauri dev`): a window in the top-left corner with the player's raw loudness, the leveling gain, the second bar's height and the drum pops, for tuning the bars. A release build doesn't contain it.
- `src-tauri/src/control/`: the control center: each button is an object with the `Control` trait, toggles work with the system through the `Switch` port, whose Windows implementations are in `control/adapters/` (device volume, sleep block). A new button is a new type and a line in `ControlCenter::standard`. A button that needs the island itself (screenshot, settings) says so through `Outcome`.
- `src-tauri/src/files/`: files whoever brought them: a file's kind by its extension (`Kind`, under tests), for the placeholder of a file without a picture (`island/view/file_icon.rs`, which any view showing a file uses); `files/adapters/`: Windows' known folders, opening a file, showing one or several files selected in Explorer. The screenshot, the recording and downloads all use it.
- `src-tauri/src/download/`: downloads (under tests): `Tracker` turns the Downloads folder's changes into what is coming (partial files and their total size) and what has arrived (a partial file renamed, or a fresh file written straight that went quiet); the site from Windows' mark on a file; `download/adapters/`: the folder watched on a thread of its own (ReadDirectoryChangesW), an arrived file's site and thumbnail read there. `examples/download_probe.rs` plays a browser, a messenger and a copy in a temp folder and prints what the watch told. On the island: `island/downloads.rs` (the watch by the setting, the progress, the arrived files and joining them, clicks, the gate) and `island/view/download.rs` (the spinner with the size, the arrow, the stack, the card and the pile). The stage kinds are `Downloading` (a state, under a recording) and `Download`.
- `src-tauri/src/capture/`: what a screenshot and a recording share (under tests): what to capture (`Source`: a monitor and a rectangle on it: the whole monitor or a selected area), the picture in memory (`Image`), the time stamp for the file name (`Stamp`), what was decided about the file (`Status`); `capture/adapters/`: the clipboard (picture, file, text), a free file name, hiding a window from captures, and `graphics.rs` (Windows.Graphics.Capture: a monitor as an item, a frame pool with a session, a frame as a texture), which the recording and an HDR screenshot both use.
- `src-tauri/src/shot/`: screenshot: `Shot` decides whether to save a file or only put it in the clipboard (covered by tests), the `Gallery` and `Clipboard` ports; `shot/edit.rs`: editor marks, edit history, pixels and crop (also under tests); `shot/adapters/`: screen capture (GDI; Graphics Capture for a monitor in HDR mode, so the colors are not washed out), PNG via WIC.
- `src-tauri/src/play/`: video playback (under tests): `Player` holds where the video is, whether it runs, its speed (0.5×–4×) and sound, a drag of the progress bar (one seek at a time, only the latest target waits), the bar's run to the end for the compositor and the moment the time text changes, the `Engine` port, another engine of the same video taking over at the same place (`replace`; no engine at all while the editor shows the edit); `play/adapters/`: Media Engine presenting on the video card into its own swap chain for a Composition surface, the frame on screen. The window is `island/viewer/video.rs`; `examples/play_probe.rs` drives a real file through it without a window.
- `src-tauri/src/montage/`: the video editor's rules (under tests), `splice.rs` the saved sound joined fragment by fragment and crossed at transitions; `Montage` holds the fragments in their order (each a source span with its speed, muted sound and zoom) and the transitions on their joins, cuts, removes and moves them, and says what a moment of the result shows; `curve.rs` the zoom and transition curves, `squeeze.rs` the compression modes and size estimates, `stretch.rs` sound sped up with its pitch kept (WSOLA), `strip.rs` the timeline's thumbnail moments and the sound's peaks, `watch.rs` watching an edit on two engines in turns (the next fragment waits on the second one, a transition plays both); `montage/adapters/`: Media Foundation reading a recording (facts, thumbnails on the video card, sound as floats) and `writer.rs` saving an edit with every effect on the video card. `examples/edit_probe.rs` measures opening and saves an edit with every effect in each compression mode, with frames of the result as PNG. The island's side of a save is `island/watch_save.rs`.
- `src-tauri/src/gpu.rs`: the Direct3D video device the recording and the player hand to Media Foundation (`Card`: one per video window, shared by its engines and the timeline's thumbnails), BGRA textures read back into memory.
- `src-tauri/src/select/`: area selection (under tests): what the pointer over a monitor picks: the lit window, a click, a drag around a window with a little room, Alt; `select/adapters/`: the windows on a monitor and their visible bounds.
- `src-tauri/src/update/`: the update rules (under tests): which version is newer, what the user may do with an offer (only a patch can be skipped; an offer can be postponed, an hour at a time, for a day after it was first shown), when a check may run (a day apart, after a minute without input and nothing important going on), the manifest and what the island remembers between runs. It also checks the installer's signature (`signature.rs`: the public key built in, the format `tauri signer` writes). The network and the installer are adapters, not built yet.
- `src-tauri/src/media/`: media sessions, covers, player loudness, app icons and file thumbnails from the shell.
- `src-tauri/src/devices/`: devices (under tests): a device's kind from Windows' category and Bluetooth classes, its picture by name (`Look`), the sound endpoint's kind and name, and `Tracker` (connected, waiting for the battery, disconnected, low at 20% and 10%, the sound switch joined to a connection); `devices/adapters/`: the paired Bluetooth devices and the nodes carrying their battery (two device watchers), the default sound endpoints (IMMNotificationClient), tied by container on a thread of their own. `examples/device_probe.rs` prints everything Windows says about devices live. On the island: `island/devices.rs` (the watch by the settings, the waits, the gate, the held notices) and `island/view/device.rs` (the large connected notice, its silver and the band of light, the battery ring, the small notices) with `island/view/device_art.rs` (the line drawings, drawn by Direct2D into a mask; the traced outlines are in `device_art/traced.rs`). The stage kinds are `DeviceConnected`, `DeviceDisconnected`, `DeviceLow`, `AudioSwitch`.
- `src-tauri/src/notify/`: system notifications (under tests): the notices and what may show now (`gate`, and `allow` for downloads: a switched-off kind, Do Not Disturb, a fullscreen app, a recording that holds the rest), `battery.rs` (low charge from Windows' Energy saver threshold every 5%, every percent from 10%, waiting for Energy saver at the first step, plugged, unplugged, charged, slow charging), `drain.rs` (the usual drain learnt on this computer and a fast drain against it), `usage.rs` (which apps hold the processor, the leader, high load); `notify/adapters/`: the battery, Windows' Energy saver threshold and the power events, every process's processor time and an app's name. `examples/power_probe.rs` reads them live. On the island: `island/notify.rs` (the readings, the minute timer, the gate, the sounds, the held notices) and `island/view/system.rs` (the small, medium and large looks, the battery built of parts the compositor moves, the gauge and the processor); the critical badge is one of the mode badges in `view/status.rs`. The stage kinds are `LowCharge`, `CriticalCharge`, `Power`, `SlowCharge`, `Usage`.
- `src-tauri/src/island/`: the island: one thread and one state (`App`).
  - `mod.rs` is the switchboard: the state, start and stop, the once-a-second tick, and which handler gets a window message or a timer. It holds no feature logic.
  - A feature per file, each with its own state and what it does on the island: `reminders.rs` (the reminder's sound and notice, pause, rewind by holding), `player.rs` ("Now playing": sessions, dropping a silent player, the bars, commands), `controls.rs` (the control center: the system parts of the buttons, what a press or a hold leads to, when the menu closes, the bottom line's clock timer; its picture is `view/strip.rs`), `screenshot.rs` (the screenshot scenario: shutter, flight, saving, viewer window), `record.rs` (the recording scenario), `watch.rs` (a recording in the video window: opening from the card, the status, back to the card, copy, delete, frame to screenshot), `record_panel.rs` (the pre-start panel: opening it, the area circle, saving the choices, listening to a microphone), `problem.rs` (errors: reporting, missed ones, showing again), `notify.rs`, `downloads.rs` and `devices.rs` (system notifications, above).
  - Shared by the screenshot and the recording: `capture.rs` (what is captured, hiding the island from captures, which of the two cards takes a click or Ctrl+C, which window the viewer status belongs to, the flights between a card and a window), `select.rs` (the area selection: a frozen screen for a screenshot, the live one for a recording, and where the picked area goes) and `hotkeys.rs` (every global key: the three from the settings, Ctrl+C and Ctrl+X while a card exists, Esc during the countdown, the selection and the panel waiting after it).
  - Shared mechanics: `input.rs` (mouse, wheel, gestures: it only decides whom to ask), `render.rs` (which view is on and the model the scene draws from), `layout.rs` (where the window sits, the click area), `visibility.rs` (fullscreen apps, hiding from the tray, dimming), `config.rs` (rereading `settings.json`, opening the settings window).
  - Drawing and Win32, which know nothing about `App`: `view.rs` (the scene: the capsule, swapping views, hover, hit testing) and `view/` (a view per file: timers, player with its level bars in `player/bars.rs`, menu, the recording's pre-start panel, screenshot shutter, the capture card of a screenshot or a recording, viewer status, recording, problem, mode badges, swipe, pull), `overlay.rs` (a window over a whole monitor on the island's compositor: what the flight, the selection and the marks share), `flight.rs` (the shot's flight window: a whole screen via the center, an area straight to the island), `selector.rs` (the area selection window: the wave from the island, dimming, the lit window, the frame, its size, the Alt hint), `marks.rs` (the corner marks around a recorded area), `viewer/` (the viewer windows: the screenshot one in `mod.rs` with its editor and drawing marks, the video one in `video.rs` with its bottom panel `transport.rs` and its editor in `montage/` (the tools `tools.rs`, the timeline `timeline.rs`, the scissors cursor `pointer.rs`, the two engines' pictures and the zoom frame `screen.rs`, the thumbnails and wave read in the background `pictures.rs`); shared: zoom and pan `lens.rs`, capsules, the top panel `bar.rs`, the signs on the picture and the spinner `signs.rs`), `glyph.rs` (vector icons), `comp.rs` (Composition and text), `tray.rs`, `window.rs`.
- `src-tauri/src/record/`: screen recording: what to record and the frame size (`Plan`), time without pauses (`Clock`), when to hand a frame to the encoder (`Pacer`), the dissolve after a pause (`Fade`), the fate of a finished recording (`Take`), the sound choice and the mixing of inputs into one track in recorded time (`sound.rs`: `Sound`, `Lane`, `Track`), what the next recording is like (`choice.rs`: quality, codec, frames per second, countdown, microphone) what a press in the pre-start panel does (`panel.rs`), the bars of the microphone it listens to (`meter.rs`), all under tests; `record/adapters/`: the recording thread (Windows.Graphics.Capture), the sound inputs (WASAPI: every program but the island, the chosen or default microphone), the list of microphones and the panel's meter (`microphones.rs`), the encoder (Media Foundation, H.264 or HEVC in MP4 with AAC), scaling to a smaller video and frame mixing for the dissolve (Direct2D), the recordings folder and the shelf for Ctrl+X. The island side is `island/record.rs` (the scenario), `island/record_panel.rs` (the panel) and `island/view/record.rs`, `island/view/panel.rs` and `island/view/microphones.rs` (the views: the recording, the panel's main page, its microphones page).
- `src-tauri/src/lang.rs` + `src-tauri/locales/`: the island's language: the `Language` setting (auto / en / ru), how Windows' language list is matched to a supported language, and the `tr!("key", name = value)` lookup (rust-i18n; the catalogs `en.json` and `ru.json` are embedded into the exe). Tests check that both catalogs have the same keys and `%{values}`, and that every key used in the code exists. A new text is a key in both catalogs and a `tr!` call; a new language is a catalog file and a `Lang` variant.
- `src-tauri/src/hotkey.rs`: a global key from the settings: place on the keyboard, label, which combinations are acceptable, and `Slot`, which keeps one such key taken from Windows in step with the settings and tells a refusal once (under tests).
- `src-tauri/src/fault.rs`: errors (under tests): what failed (`What`, a closed list, each with a level, a code and a title), why (`Cause`, from a Windows code), the titles and reason texts (from the language catalog). Adapters return `Failed` (the cause and the system's answer) and print nothing themselves; the island builds a `Fault` and reports it in one place, `island/problem.rs` (`App::report`: journal, notice, missed ones, replay; from other threads through `Reporter`). The view is `island/view/problem.rs`. A new error is a line in `What` and a `report` call.
- `src-tauri/src/journal/`: the `island.log` journal; `entries.rs` reads it back into entries for the settings window (under tests: a problem's line is parsed by its code, so its title and reason come in the window's language); `src-tauri/src/crash.rs`: what to do when the process goes down: write to the journal, close the screen-recording file, restart the island.
- `src-tauri/src/platform.rs`: WinAPI: idle, fullscreen mode, monitors, autostart, single instance, hotkeys and PrtSc in Snipping Tool, the display-language list.
- `src-tauri/windows/hooks.nsh`: the installer's question about PrtSc (in the installer's language); the installer languages are in `tauri.conf.json`.
- `src-tauri/src/sound.rs`: sound synthesis (a voice per tone: notes, overtones, decay, a pitch glide) and playback via PlaySound. The tone names are `settings::TONES`.
- `src-tauri/src/persist.rs`: file paths and saving timers.
- `src-tauri/src/update_app.rs` + `src/update`: the update window (the exe with `--update`, on Tauri like the settings window). It owns nothing: it shows `update-status.json` (the notes, the choices, the download) and sends the island commands; "About" in the settings does the same through the same two commands.
- `src-tauri/src/island/update.rs` (+ `view/update.rs`): the owner of the update: it checks (by the clock or on a command), keeps the state and writes `update-status.json`, downloads and installs, and shows it with the spinner, the check mark, the cards and the problem notices. A manual check from the settings looks the same as one by the clock; one pressed during the clock's check takes it over. `scripts/release.mjs` signs and describes a release.
- `src-tauri/src/settings_app.rs` + `src/settings`: the settings window. `SettingsPage.tsx` loads and saves (a moment after the last change), `Navigator.tsx` slides the pages, `state.tsx` is what every page reads, `ui.tsx` the rows and controls, `icons.tsx` the glyphs, `pages/` one file per group of pages. Its texts are in `src/locales/` (i18next; keys are checked by `tsc`, a key missing in one language doesn't compile), set up in `src/i18n.ts`.

Files in `%APPDATA%\com.greatisland.app`: `settings.json` (and its copy `settings.backup.json`), `timers.json`, `battery.json` (the usual battery drain), the `island.log` journal.

## Build

```
npm install
npx tauri build                      # exe and installer in src-tauri/target/release/bundle/nsis
cd src-tauri && cargo test           # logic tests: timers, gestures, ribbon, player
```

Version history is in [CHANGELOG.en.md](CHANGELOG.en.md) (English) and [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md) (Russian, the original).

A release: `node scripts/release.mjs "<commit message>"` from the root. It asks for the version and the key password, bumps the version, runs the checks, builds the installer, signs it with the private key (kept outside the repository, `%USERPROFILE%\.great-island\update.key`), writes the update manifest `latest.json`, checks the signature the way the island does, then commits, pushes, tags and publishes the release on GitHub.

The installer is not signed: on launch Windows shows SmartScreen, "More info" → "Run anyway".
